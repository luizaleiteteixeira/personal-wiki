**MINI-EXPERIENCE CASE**

**OPENING WEEK AT DARDEN**

**Martin N. Davidson & Melissa C. Thomas-Hunt (2019)**

**1) From a diversity, equity, inclusion, and belonging perspective, what are the problems these students are facing?**

The class seems diverse (at least based on this sample) in terms of background, nationality, gender, and sexual orientation. However, even though everyone was interested in meeting new and different people, the students struggled with a lack of inclusion and sense of belonging, especially due to language barriers, cultural differences, fear of being misinterpreted, and limited psychological safety.

For me, the main problem is the lack of communication. Students are making assumptions and avoiding conversations because they are afraid of offending others or being judged. But, as we discussed in class, silence only increases distance and misunderstandings.

**2) What do you think the long term effects of these experiences might be, on the students and the school?**

The effect would be homophily: students will interact only with those who share similar backgrounds, nationalities, and experiences. Instead of learning from different perspectives and cultures, they will stay within their “bubbles”, reducing creativity, growth and innovation.

For the students, this limits their learning. They will participate less in discussions, feel less comfortable taking risks, and miss opportunities to learn more about other cultures. For the school, the lack of inclusion can damage the climate and reputation. If students feel excluded or unsupported, they are less likely to speak positively about the program. For example, prospective applicants from underrepresented groups or non-native English speakers may decide not to apply based on their colleagues' experience, which will lead to less diverse classes in the following years. 

**3) From the perspective of an inclusive leader, what are possible long-term solutions for the issues these MBAs are facing, and for those that future students might similarly face?**

The first thing that came to my mind was communication. As a leader, I would bring everyone together to have an open conversation where people can share what they’re feeling, what challenges they’re facing, and where they’re coming from. It’s important to create a psychologically safe environment so everyone feels comfortable speaking their minds without fear of judgment. Encouraging vulnerability and perspective-taking (as we discussed in Leading People) helps build empathy and understanding. Once everyone’s perspective is shared, the group can set commitments on how to support one another. Each student has different strengths that can help others. For example, someone great in Finance could help Jocelyn with quantitative topics, while she could support Xiao with English. 

There are also more tactical things the school can do: offering language and participation support for non-native English speakers (such as creating ways for students to share reflections in writing if they’re not yet comfortable speaking), ensuring that the faculty and staff themselves reflect diversity (and talk about their own challenges so students can relate and learn), and, of course, courses like Business Communication in Diverse Work Environments can help students practice these conversations.

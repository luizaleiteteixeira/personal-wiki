## **Complex Interactions**

### **Session 8: Power**

* **Definition:** **Asymmetric control of resources and outcomes**.  
  * From a position (formal authority), relationship (“informal”) or personal (expertise/competence)  
* **Milgram Experiment:** shocking people cuz the “scientist” said to. people obey “legitimate” authority, especially under pressure or ambiguity, and structured situations.   
* **Signals of Power:** Title, clothing, confidence, obedience from others, physical traits. People infer power from appearance → these signals can mislead or be innacurate (Milgram’s lab coat).  
  * Perception of power leads to anchoring  
* **Effects of Power:**  
  * Increases independent focus: confidence, less inhibition, more risk-taking.  
  * Decreases interdependent focus: less empathy and perspective-taking.  
  * **Power Amplification Effect**: Every subtle gesture (silence, ambiguity, criticism) is magnified by subordinates → Benign comment → criticism; quick thanks → feels huge.  
  * **Ethical risk: Noble Cause Corruption**: convincing others that "the ends justify the means." \- does something unethical while believing it serves a greater good  
* **Counteracting Negative Effects:** Cultivate **perspective-taking** and a **beginner's mindset** (rediscovery).  
  * Leaders must counteract with clarity, attention, empathy, praise publicly / criticize privately, and anonymous feedback channels, shortening long meetings → create psychologic safety spaces  
* People tend to follow power, but not overabuse. In some situations, power cannot be used \- focus on negotiation, persuasion, network, culture..  
* The what, why and how matter\!

### **Session 9: Inclusive Leadership (DEI)**

* **Diversity \=** Presence of a .docx of identities or characteristics in organizations  
* **Equity \=** Pursuing fairness with respect to historically marginalized communities  
* **Inclusion \=** Creating a culture that promotes respect  
* Considerations in Hiring, Promotion, and Exit  
  * Hiring the right “fit” is often used but should be measured, not subjective which could lead to biases (people similar to you or who look like prior successes)  
  * Evidence: whitened resumes, racial disparities in discipline and arbitration  
* Hierarchy is formed from **ascribed/ascriptive** (cannot change, don't have control \- race, gender, background) and **achieved** (reflect individual choices \- degrees, titles).   
  * Characteristics → Association/beliefs → how you see people  
* **Definition:** Building **systems that enable groups to perform to their full potential**. The focus is on **systems**, not just fighting individual biases.  
* **Organizational Best Practices:**  
  * **Contact:** social networks  
  * **Training**: cognitive, emotion or perspective-taking interventions  
  * **Categorization:** Build inclusive systems and shared identity (teams, mission)  
  * Others: Assign **all managers** to be agents of change; build inclusive systems, integrate work groups, utilize **social accountability** with clear metrics to achieve your goals  
* **Strategic Alignment:**   
  * Build trust via psychological safety, systems to reduce bias  
  * DEI policies work best when they are **embedded in core business operations**, not treated as a separate, optional initiative.  
  * Corporate social initiatives cannot ignore broader political environment  
  * Clear metrics and accountability are needed  
  * Terms and frameworks must adapt to changing cultural contexts  
  * Economic benefits need to be demonstrable and compelling

### **Session 10: Persuasion**

* **Goal:** Bringing disparate parties together in pursuit of a shared vision or convincing them to change → persuasion leads to motivation.  
* **Steps for a Persuasive Message:**  
  * Define your goal (and whether you’re the right messenger).  
  * Know your audience → their interests, coalitions, alternatives.  
  * Build logical \+ emotional arguments with evidence.  
  * Stand on common ground (shared values).  
  * State the problem \+ solution/choice clearly.  
  * Paint a vivid vision of the future.  
  * End with a call to action / positive ask.  
* **Cialdini's Six Principles of Social Influence (Tactics):**  
  * **Authority**: People tend to comply with those they perceive as experts or legitimate authorities → establish expertise and cite credible sources.  
    * Defense: Ask — Is this authority actually relevant and competent in this context?  
  * **Scarcity**: People assign higher value to things that appear rare or time-limited (fomo)→ emphasize limited-time / exclusive opportunities. Framing as a loss  
    * Defense: Pause to consider whether the offer truly meets your needs or if you’re reacting to pressure.  
  * **Social Proof**: We look to others’ behavior to guide our own decisions, especially under uncertainty → show that others are already taking the desired action, and share success stories.  
    * Defense: Ask — Are these “others” credible and comparable to me?  
  * **Liking**: We’re more likely to say yes to people we like or who make us feel liked → build genuine relationships (use compliments and show interest by asking questions).  
    * Defense: Separate the message from the messenger — Am I agreeing because I like them, or because it makes sense?  
  * **Commitment & Consistency**: Once people commit publicly or in writing, they’re motivated to behave consistently with that stance → secure a small, public commitment first (manager first ask to review a draft and later request full help on a project)  
    * Defense: Reflect before agreeing — Does this commitment still fit my priorities?  
  * **Reciprocity**: People feel obligated to return favors or concessions they’ve received → Offer genuine help or value before asking for something in return  
    * Defense: Acknowledge the tactic — gratitude is good, obligation is optional.  
* Other strategies  
  * State what people tend to lose (don't like losing → take action)  
* Ethical persuasion → win-win alignment.

### **Session 11: Negotiations**

* **Definition:** A decision-making process by which 2+ people agree how to allocate scarce resources.  
* Phases:   
  * Prepare   
    * Establish Interests and develop a Scoring System: Quantify issues with weights for prioritization.  
    * Establish your bargaining points: BATNA, Reservation price, target price  
    * Understand who you are negotiating with (interest goals) and who they represent  
    * Plan & practice (questions and anticipate reaction)  
  * Negotiate  
    * Distributive issues: Single issue negotiation (win-lose/zero sum) \- you value things in exactly the inverse way as your counterpart.  
    * Compatible issues: both parties want the same thing \- outcome is good for both parties  
    * Gather other side’s Information: Ask open-ended questions to uncover the other side's true interests. Perspective taking  
    * Make Offers:   
      * Make an ambitious first offer (not aggressive) \- anchor (persuasion advantage).   
        * If you can make an educated guess on other parties BATNA \- go first (persuasion advantage)  
      * Go second if want information (info advantage)  
      * Make concessions in trade-offs, not unilaterally.  
    * Tips: build trust, ask questions, give away information, make multiple offers simultaneously  
  * Finalize   
    * Go back and re-discuss if needed  
    * Maintain trust  
  * Fulfill  
* **Key Terms:**  
  * **Positions:** The stated request \- public claim of the negotiator (**"WHAT"** they say they want).  
  * **Interests:** The underlying need \- private interests behind the position (**"WHY"** they want it).  
  * **BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement):** Your greatest source of power; your walk-away plan (plan B).   
    * FATNA \= fantasized alternative, but not an actual option  
  * **Reservation Price:** Your walk-away point; the minimum acceptable deal. BATNA \+ opportunity costs/switching costs  
  * **Target Price:** realistic but very positive goal of where you’d like to end up (SMART)  
  * **ZOPA (zone of possible agreement):** overlap of buyer’s target & reservation price with the seller’s

## **Macro Interactions**

### **Session 12: Social Networks**

* Web of relationships that transfer information, influence, and opportunities. Leaders use networks to get things done, learn, and mobilize resources  
* **Network Types:**  
  * **Expansive Network (Efficiency):** Characterized by weak ties and bridges to other networks. Benefits include diverse/unique information, creativity and informal power.  
  * **Cohesive Network (Ideation):** Characterized by strong ties and redundant contacts. Benefits include high trust and speed to consensus/cooperation.  
* **Structural Holes** are the gaps between non-redundant contacts. **Brokers** who bridge these holes have power due to access to unique information.  
* **Building an Effective Network:** Seek diversity, maximize efficiency (mix of tie types)  
* **Maintaining an Effective Network:** GAFA \- gratitude, acknowledgements (tell when good things happen, offer help when bad things, favors (reciprocity), and providing advice (sharing a bit of you)  
* **Build coalitions (agreement \+ trust):** united for a single purpose  
  * ****  
    * Allies → bring new info, perspective and connections → work with.  
    * Bedfellows → can help build support → work with  
    * Opponents → productive disagreement → debate with.  
    * Adversaries → avoid.  
    * Fence-sitters → don’t waste time.  
* **Value of networking:** reduce homophily (The tendency for personal and professional networks to be homogeneous with regard to many sociodemographic, behavioral, and intrapersonal characteristics)  
* **Cumulative advantage:** "rich get richer” → choices or preferences get amplified in social networks

### **Session 13: Culture**

* **Definition:** The shared pattern of beliefs, values, symbols, and behavioral expectations (norms).  
  * Normative order \- right/wrong way  
  * Cognitive order \- explanations of process/ways of the world  
* **Purpose:** provides control, meaning and consistency without micromanaging  
* **Why does culture work?**  
  * Control: Provides consistent decision rules for managers (shortcuts).  
  * Motivation: Acts as a powerful intrinsic motivator, providing identity and meaning.  
  * Shared Goals: facilitate cooperation  
  * Differentiation: Offers a difficult-to-replicate strategic advantage.  
* **Layers:**  
  * **Artifacts (visible or audible expressions):** Mission statements, symbols, stories, heroes, ceremonies/rituals   
    * They are practices that express deeper values  
    * observable manifestations, not the underlying values themselves  
  * **Values (principles and beliefs):** aspirations, goals, ideals, norms  
  * **Assumptions**: deep, taken for granted beliefs  
* **Strong vs. Positive Culture:** A **Strong Culture** is intensely held, widely shared, and consistently displayed. A strong culture is **not inherently positive** and can be exploited.  
* **Tools for Managing Culture:**  
  * Mission statements: link strategy to motivation \- has to be easy to remember.  
  * Symbols: visual identity.  
  * Stories: make values memorable \- examples.  
  * Heroes: role models, represent culture.  
  * Ceremonies: celebrate shared successes, reward people.  
* Competing Values Framework (CVR)  
  *   
* There is no single best culture — the best one fits your strategy & stage  
* Jenny  
  * Culture: strategic relevant, strong, adaptive overtime (when strategy changes, behavior has to follow)  
* Strong cultures that were once an advantage can become a barrier if they are misaligned with a changing external environment.

### **Session 14: Change**

* Organizational transformation in structure, strategy, operations, or culture to adapt to pressures. Can be incremental or radical.  
* **Challenge:** People tend to resist new ideas and ideas. Change requires measured, intentional action.  
  * **Status quo bias**: Preference for the current state of affairs  
  * **Loss aversion**   
  * **Endowment effect:** once you have ownership over something, you value it more  
* **3 stages of change: Unfreeze** existing practices, **change** through communication & support, **refreeze** by enforcing or supporting new behaviors after the change  
* **For successful change:**   
  * Treat change as continuous.  
  * Embed it into operating rhythm and culture.  
  * Manage energy levels and expectations – know when organization can absorb change.  
  * Use aspirations to stretch thinking.  
  * Drive change from the middle out.  
  * Build social capital and spend it wisely.

# Examples in the Wild 4

At Egon Zehnder, we faced challenges with third-degree price discrimination when clients compared fees and realized they were being charged different prices. To address this, we introduced a “menu of services” that allowed clients to self-select the option that best matched their willingness to pay. Instead of offering only the “full recruiting project” at a high fee, we created a new product: “strategic introductions.” In this model, we presented only the top five candidates we had interviewed and limited the level of market mapping. Although both services had similar marginal costs, they were framed differently to appeal to distinct client segments.

This is an example of versioning in second-degree price discrimination. It was profitable because the "high type” clients, such as large corporations, had a higher willingness to pay for the upgrade (comprehensive service), while "low type” clients, like startups, started to work with us as they could now afford the lower-priced version. Clients revealed their willingness to pay during early discussions by selecting between packages, allowing us to expand our customer base and increase revenue without openly charging different prices for the same service.

**Case: Boris Groysberg & Colleen Ammerman (2021). Glass-Shattering Leaders: Jack Rivkin case**

1) In your eyes, what are the most effective steps that Rivkin took to increase diversity?

First, to treat everyone the same. He believed in people's potential. Since the selective process, he created an environment where people felt seen and not judged. This same environment was kept once people joined: supportive, with opportunities to grow, and autonomy and flexibility for people to work at their best self. The culture Rivkin created (no-jerk and zero tolerance for disrespect or biased behavior) was also important for people to feel safe. 

2) What are drawbacks of Rivkin's approach?

That everything depended too much on one person. The environment Rivkin created was very connected to him, so when he left, all the changes basically disappeared, and other people started to leave as well as the culture was not the same. In summary, Rivkin was not able to influence the leadership or the company as a whole to actually change. His team operated with this new and great mindset, but this was not representative of the rest of the company, that basically stayed the same. Because of that, his approach didn't last long.

3) How would you improve upon Rivkin's approach?

I would try to institutionalize all these practices across the entire company and its different day-to-day activities, such as the recruiting process, no-jerk culture, feedback… That means putting these ideas into the HR systems and making sure everyone follows the same standards and values. I would do it in a top-down approach, where senior leadership is on board and committed, so then everyone embraces the culture and it lasts longer as it doesn't depend solely on one person.

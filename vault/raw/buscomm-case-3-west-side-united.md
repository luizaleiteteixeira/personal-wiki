**1) Are the changes Dr. Ansell and colleagues are using at WSU to change the healthcare of the healthcare delivery system and community investment enough to make a difference?**

The changes WSU is trying to implement are targeting a structural problem that has been part of society for a long time. So I think what they are doing is a great start, especially because it helps shift the mindset and pushes people to look at health and racial inequality with a different lens. It creates more awareness and starts to move the system in a better direction. At the same time however, for this to really make a long-term difference, it has to grow a lot, and many more people and institutions need to be on board. WSU is only one part of a much bigger change that is required. 

In summary, it won’t be enough to completely solve the problem alone, but it marks an important start that can open doors for others to join and increase the impact.

**2) Assess WSU's accomplishments as well as the gaps in the organizations. If you were Bill Gates, or the head of another major foundation dedicated to health equity, would you fund this program? Why or why not?**

Accomplishments: They were able to build trust with the community (especially during challenging times such as the pandemic), increase local hiring, invest in local small businesses, and create career pathways for students. They also coordinated hospitals that were used to compete with each other. 

Gaps: The structure is not fully stable yet, because it still depends a lot on funding, volunteers, and on how committed the hospitals and their leaders are. I also think they need to prioritize better, since they are trying to tackle very big and structural problems while still being a small organization.

Despite the gaps, I would fund the program, as I truly believe in their mission. They have shown that they can generate great impact and they have the potential to continue growing and improving.

**3) How well did Ansell and colleagues assemble a coalition and turn it into an organization? What vulnerabilities or gaps could interfere with the ability of WSU to continue its work?**

I believe they did a good job because they were able to bring together the major hospitals, create a shared governance structure, and align their goals with one shared mission. At the same time, WSU still depends a lot on the commitment of hospital leadership, volunteers and funding, which is challenging especially after moments of budget constraints like the pandemic. With this, their capacity to grow is still limited, but with potential.

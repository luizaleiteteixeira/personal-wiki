**1) How should Morrison and the board move forward with their goal of having at least 50% of the Equinox directors be women or people of color? Should they abandon their goal, maintain their goal, or moderate their approach? If so, how?**

The board performs better because of the diversity they brought, and they had already made a huge progress to achieve the target of at least 50% women or people of color. So, I don't think they should abandon the goal, but rephrase how they talk about it publicly. In Leading People class, we had a speaker who said that her title changed from “Diversity Manager” to something more neutral, related to “People,” even though her job was still the same. It was just communicated differently to avoid unnecessary pushback.

I believe Equinox can do something similar. They can keep working toward a more diverse board and keep the same intention behind the scenes, but change the way they present the goal to the public. Instead of saying “we only consider women and minorities,” they can say that they use a skills-based process with a diverse slate of candidates. 

In summary the actions don’t need to change, but the communication can.

**2) What should Morrison do specifically?**

I think Morrison should acknowledge that the wording of the original mandate was too strong, but she shouldn’t back away from the goal itself. She can reframe the policy in a way that feels more neutral and less confrontational, while keeping the main idea behind it. From our previous discussions about confrontation, we talked about the power of sharing personal feelings and using empathy to build understanding. Morrison has a real story to talk about, as she struggled to get onto boards herself, even though she was extremely qualified. She could share how that experience shaped her and why improving diversity matters to her personally, not just as a “policy.” This could help others empathize and understand the intention behind the actions. She can also state how the board has already improved, showing numbers and examples of better performance since they diversified. That makes the conversation more grounded in results. 

After that, I agree that she should revise the policy’s language and bring it back to the board, focusing on inclusive slates and strong qualifications, not on specific groups. Once things calm down a bit and the situation is less “hot”, she can also talk with the shareholder group and other stakeholders, explaining the new approach in a clearer and more thoughtful way.

**3) Is there a path forward that allows for everyone to win, and if not, who should make what sacrifice and why?**

I don’t think there is a perfect win-win for everyone. Someone will have to give something up. In this case, I believe the board should adjust the approach but not the goal. The shareholder group won’t get everything they want, and that’s okay. The “sacrifice” should be the strict wording of the mandate, not the commitment to diversity. Morrison can update the language to make it more balanced and less exclusive, while still keeping the same mission. The shareholder group will also need to accept that Equinox will continue investing in diversity because it makes the board stronger.

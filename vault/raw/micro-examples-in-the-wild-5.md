# Examples in the Wild 5

I was working at Nestlé in the Chocolate business unit when the pandemic started. We quickly realized that consumer demand in Brazil was shifting from out-of-home consumption to home cooking. To respond, we created a new segment called Homebaking to capture consumers experimenting in their kitchens. We launched products such as cookie and brownie dough, chocolate chips, cocoa powder, and other ingredients that could be easily incorporated into homemade recipes.

This made Nestlé the first mover in the category, building strong consumer awareness and loyalty. Sales grew fast as families adopted baking as both a routine activity and a way to connect. We marketed our products around family and tradition, leveraging Nestlé’s existing reputation in Brazil for products like condensed milk. Consumers began associating our products with happiness and togetherness.

Although barriers to entry were low and competitors soon launched similar offerings, our early response and branding gave Nestlé a great advantage - at least for a while. By establishing itself as the go-to option for home baking, Nestlé secured market share and consumer trust, showing how strategic thinking can create competitive advantage even in industries where long-run profits tend to zero.

## Data and Decisions

- [[AB Testing And Two-Sample Testing]]: A/B testing, or Randomized Control Trial (RCT), is a procedure using randomization to establish causation between treatments and control groups.
- [[Confidence Interval Estimation]]: A confidence interval provides a range within which the true population mean is likely to lie, based on sample data.
- [[Dummy Variables for Categorical Data]]: Categorical variables can be converted into dummy or indicator variables, typically taking values of 0 or 1.
- [[Hypothesis Testing Procedure]]: Hypothesis testing is a method used to analyze sample data to determine if there is enough evidence to challenge the existing belief or status quo.
- [[Linear Regression Model]]: Linear regression models the linear relationship between two variables, predicting one variable (Y) as a function of another (X).
- [[Multivariate Regression Analysis]]: Multivariate regression models allow for the analysis of relationships where the slope of a variable must account for the influence of other independent variables.
- [[Regression With Interactions]]: This method involves including interaction terms in a regression model to analyze differences between groups.
- [[Statistical Process Control Limits]]: Control limits are used to check if a process is functioning correctly when population parameters are known.
- [[Statistical Sampling Methods]]: Statistical sampling involves using a subset of data to make inferences about a larger population when a full census is impractical.

## Finance

- [[Basic Bond Characteristics]]: A bond represents a borrowing agreement where the issuer provides an IOU to the investor.
- [[Bond Characteristics And Yields]]: This section outlines the characteristics of bonds, including different types, cash flows, and pricing conventions.
- [[Bond Price Interest Rate Sensitivity]]: The price of a bond is the present value of its future cash flows, discounted at its Yield to Maturity (YTM).
- [[Capital Market Line Concepts]]: The Capital Market Line (CML) represents the optimal risk-return possibilities achievable by combining risk-free savings with the Market Portfolio.
- [[Equity Financing and Firm Value]]: The announcement of a project immediately increases the equity value of a firm by the Net Present Value (NPV) of that project.
- [[Estimating Asset Beta]]: Beta measures the sensitivity of a stock or project to the overall market.
- [[Firm Valuation Approaches]]: Firm value is determined by the cash flows generated by all its assets, discounted by a rate that reflects the risk of those assets.
- [[Free Cash Flow Calculation]]: Free Cash Flow (FCF) represents the cash flow available to the firm after accounting for its investment needs and taxes.
- [[Internal Rate Of Return]]: The Internal Rate of Return (IRR) is the discount rate that makes a project's Net Present Value (NPV) equal to zero.
- [[Leverage and Taxes]]: Leverage can increase firm value through the interest tax shield, but this benefit must be weighed against the direct and indirect costs associated with financial distress.
- [[Portfolio Risk and Return]]: When evaluating risky investments, the probability distribution of potential returns allows for the calculation of the expected return.
- [[Portfolio Theory Concepts]]: Portfolio theory helps determine the expected return and volatility of a combination of assets.
- [[Risk and Cost of Capital]]: Most investments carry risk, and because investors are risk-averse, they require higher returns to be compensated for undertaking that risk.

## Leading People

- [[Authentic Leadership Foundations]]: Authentic leadership stems from a combination of self-awareness and values-based decision-making.
- [[Biases in Decision Making]]: Wise decisions require overcoming cognitive limitations, as humans often rely on mental shortcuts that introduce systematic biases.
- [[Group Dynamics And Team Success]]: Teams can succeed by aggregating information and leveraging diverse expertise, but they can also fail due to homogeneity or groupthink.
- [[Inclusive Leadership and Equity]]: Inclusive leadership involves building systems that allow diverse groups to reach their full potential.
- [[Leadership Versus Management]]: Management focuses on the execution of processes and coordination, while leadership centers on providing vision and influence.
- [[Modern Management Practices]]: Modern management requires leaders to move beyond automated tasks and focus on uniquely human skills like judgment and coaching.
- [[Motivational Drivers and Goals]]: Motivation can be understood through Expectancy Theory, which links effort to performance, performance to reward, and the value of the reward.
- [[Negotiation Decision Making Process]]: Negotiation is a decision-making process where two or more individuals agree on how to allocate scarce resources.
- [[Organizational Change Management]]: Organizational transformation involves adapting structure, strategy, operations, or culture to external pressures.
- [[Organizational Culture Dynamics]]: Culture is a shared pattern of beliefs, values, and symbols that dictates behavioral expectations within an organization.
- [[Principles of Persuasion]]: Persuasion is the act of bringing different parties together toward a shared vision or convincing them to adopt a new viewpoint, which drives motivation.
- [[Social Network Dynamics]]: Social networks are webs of relationships that facilitate the transfer of information, influence, and opportunities.
- [[Understanding Dynamics of Power]]: Power is defined as the asymmetric control over resources and outcomes, which can stem from formal roles, relationships, or personal attributes.

## Marketing

- [[21Seeds Growth Strategy]]: 21Seeds successfully grew by targeting women interested in lighter, accessible cocktail options, establishing a strong lifestyle niche.
- [[A Whole New Mind]]: In an abundance economy, services that are highly experiential and emotionally driven command greater value.
- [[Brand Management Tension]]: Brand building is a long-term endeavor, but modern business pressures often demand short-term quarterly results.
- [[Brand Positioning Strategy]]: Positioning refers to the specific place a brand holds within the consumer's mind.
- [[Brand Strategy And Architecture]]: A brand is a collection of symbols and experiences that exists separately from the product itself.
- [[Category Management Strategies]]: Category management involves strategic decisions regarding a product portfolio, including choices about which products to maintain and how to grow the offering.
- [[Cause And Political Marketing]]: This section outlines considerations for cause marketing, political marketing, and managing boycotts.
- [[Cause Marketing Strategy]]: Brands must integrate sustainability and social values into their market presence to remain relevant.
- [[Decision Quality vs Outcome]]: The quality of a decision should be evaluated based on the reasoning and information used at the time, rather than solely on the final outcome.
- [[Decision-Making Across Cultures]]: Different cultures approach decision-making using distinct styles regarding hierarchy, speed, and consensus.
- [[Distribution Channel Structures]]: Distribution channels use intermediaries to connect producers with buyers, adding value through functions like access and logistics.
- [[Flag Manufacturer Growth Strategy]]: AFS, a premium flag manufacturer, seeks ways to expand beyond its main business by offering related products and services.
- [[Freemium Business Model Success]]: The freemium model offers basic features for free while gating advanced functionalities behind a paid subscription.
- [[Go-To-Market Strategy]]: Go-to-Market (GTM) strategy defines how a product is introduced, distributed, communicated, and scaled to its target audience.
- [[Levels of Product Offering]]: This model describes the different layers of value a product provides to the customer, starting from the fundamental need it fulfills up to extra services.
- [[Lexicon Branding Strategy]]: Naming strategy involves creating a brand name that is memorable and legally protectable.
- [[Market Research Methods]]: Market research bridges the gap between a business decision and the information required to support it.
- [[Market Segmentation Strategy]]: Segmentation is the process of dividing a total market into smaller, meaningful, and actionable groups.
- [[Marketing Foundational Concepts]]: Marketing is the process of creating, communicating, and delivering value to a specific target audience.
- [[Marketing Imagination Concepts]]: Successful marketing involves redefining the problem being addressed rather than simply selling a product.
- [[Nike Consumer Direct Offense]]: Nike is transitioning toward a Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) model, which allows it to control data, margins, and brand experience by decreasing reliance on wholesale partners.
- [[Political Marketing Lessons]]: Political campaigns offer parallels to marketing principles, particularly in how to reach and persuade a target audience.
- [[Porters Competitive Advantage Framework]]: This framework assesses a company's ability to achieve a competitive advantage by looking at the attractiveness of the market and the company's relative position within it.
- [[Pricing Strategies And Models]]: Pricing decisions involve balancing cost structures with consumer perception of value.
- [[Product Differentiation And Positioning]]: Positioning refers to the specific mental space a product holds in consumers' minds compared to rivals.
- [[Product Life Cycle Stages]]: The Product Life Cycle (PLC) is a model used for strategic planning that describes the stages a product goes through from introduction to decline.
- [[Product Marketing Mix Elements]]: The marketing mix encompasses several key elements including Product, Pricing, Distribution, and Promotion, each requiring strategic alignment.
- [[Product Vision Core Principle]]: Product vision requires an obsessive focus on both the product design and the overall user experience.
- [[Promotion and Communications Strategy]]: Promotion objectives must be measurable and focused on driving specific behavioral changes.
- [[Pulse Fitness Segment Evaluation]]: Pulse Fitness analyzed three customer segments to determine which offered the best long-term value.
- [[Scaling Infused Tequila Brand]]: Diageo acquired a successful infused tequila brand and must now decide on growth strategies to balance the founder's vision with the corporation's mandate for scale.
- [[Selecting Target Markets]]: Targeting involves deciding which specific group of customers to pursue.
- [[Shake Shack Product Strategy]]: Shake Shack faces a strategic decision regarding its core offering amidst shifting consumer preferences toward alternatives to beef.
- [[Sources of Competitive Advantage]]: Competitive advantage in marketing can stem from several sources, including product differentiation, brand strength, and operational efficiencies.
- [[Sources of Market Differentiation]]: Differentiation is a strategy that can be applied through any of the marketing mix elements, not solely the product itself.
- [[Strategic Marketing Frameworks]]: This section outlines various frameworks useful for strategic planning, competitive analysis, and product management.
- [[Teams Versus Zoom Comparison]]: The comparison between Microsoft Teams and Zoom illustrates how platform dynamics can outweigh individual product advantages.
- [[Tepper School Rebranding Case]]: The Tepper School underwent a complete branding refresh to clearly define its identity at the intersection of analytics and leadership.
- [[Vans Sustainability Strategy]]: Vans is evaluating whether to deepen its sustainability efforts to resonate with Gen Z consumers.

## Microeconomics

- [[Auction Types And Strategy]]: Auctions involve different payment rules depending on the type, with the optimal bidding strategy varying significantly between first-price and second-price formats.
- [[Economic Cost Concepts]]: Economic cost involves expenditures minus sunk costs and opportunity costs, representing the true cost of making a decision.
- [[Game Theory Concepts]]: Game theory analyzes decision-making in situations where outcomes depend on the choices of multiple players.
- [[Marginal Cost Estimation]]: Pricing advisory projects is challenging because accurately measuring the marginal cost is difficult.
- [[Market Power and Elasticity]]: Market power refers to a firm's ability to influence the market price, which is dependent on the product's uniqueness and the demand curve.
- [[Monopolistic Competition Market Structure]]: Monopolistic competition involves many sellers offering differentiated products in a market with low barriers to entry.
- [[Nestlé Homebaking Market Entry]]: Nestlé successfully identified a shift in consumer behavior during the pandemic, moving demand from out-of-home consumption to home cooking in Brazil.
- [[Oligopoly Market Structure]]: Oligopoly is a market structure characterized by a small number of competitors facing high barriers to entry.
- [[Price Discrimination Strategies]]: Price discrimination involves charging different prices to different consumers without those differences being due to cost variations.
- [[Price Elasticity of Demand]]: Price elasticity measures how sensitive the quantity demanded is to a change in price.
- [[Simple Monopoly Pricing]]: A monopolist determines its pricing strategy by understanding the relationship between price, quantity, and revenue.
- [[Sunk Cost Reasoning Example]]: Sunk cost reasoning occurs when past, unrecoverable costs influence current decisions.
- [[Third-Degree Pricing Discrimination]]: Egon Zehnder introduced third-degree pricing discrimination to cater to different client types, allowing them to charge varying fees based on company size and segment.
- [[Two-Part Tariff Structure]]: A pricing strategy involving an initial fee and a per-unit price, which allows the seller to capture all the value created in the transaction.

# Portfolio Risk and Return

When evaluating risky investments, the probability distribution of potential returns allows for the calculation of the expected return. Risk is measured by how spread out these possible outcomes are, which is quantified using variance and standard deviation.

## Key points

- The expected return is a weighted average of possible returns, weighted by their probabilities.
- Low risk corresponds to returns being closely clustered around the expected value.
- Variance is the expected squared deviation from the mean, and standard deviation is the square root of the variance.
- Diversification reduces risk exposure by combining assets whose outcomes are not perfectly correlated.
- Investors are compensated only for risks that cannot be diversified away (systematic risk).
- Idiosyncratic risk is company-specific, while systematic risk relates to the broader economy.

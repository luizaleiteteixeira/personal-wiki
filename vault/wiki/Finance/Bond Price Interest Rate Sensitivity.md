# Bond Price Interest Rate Sensitivity

The price of a bond is the present value of its future cash flows, discounted at its Yield to Maturity (YTM). Bond prices and YTM have an inverse relationship, meaning as YTM rises, the bond price falls.

## Key points

- The price of a bond converges to its face value as it matures.
- The effect of discounting is most dramatic for long-term bonds.
- The longer the maturity, the more sensitive the bond is to interest rate changes.
- Zero-coupon bonds are more sensitive to interest rate changes than coupon bonds.
- A 30-year zero-coupon bond is highly sensitive to yield changes.
- The overall return equals the YTM only if the bond is held to maturity.

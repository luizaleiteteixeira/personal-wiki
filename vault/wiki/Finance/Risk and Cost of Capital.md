# Risk and Cost of Capital

Most investments carry risk, and because investors are risk-averse, they require higher returns to be compensated for undertaking that risk. The cost of capital represents the minimum return a firm must provide to its investors for assuming the project's risks.

## Key points

- The required return or cost of capital is calculated as the Risk-Free rate plus a Risk Interest Rate Premium.
- Higher risk is associated with higher average returns, which translates to a higher cost for the firm.
- When discounting expected free cash flows, the discount rate must compensate investors for the risk they are taking.
- Cash flows received sooner are discounted at a lower rate than those received later.
- Safer cash flows require a lower discount rate compared to riskier cash flows.

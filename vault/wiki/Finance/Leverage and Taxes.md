# Leverage and Taxes

Leverage can increase firm value through the interest tax shield, but this benefit must be weighed against the direct and indirect costs associated with financial distress. The Trade-Off theory suggests that firms balance the tax benefits of debt against the costs of potential bankruptcy.

## Key points

- Interest expenses are tax-deductible, while dividends are not.
- The Interest Tax Shield reduces taxes paid by the firm.
- The present value of future interest tax shields increases firm value.
- Direct bankruptcy costs include legal and accounting fees.
- Indirect costs involve loss of intangibles and strained relationships.
- The Trade-Off theory balances debt tax benefits against financial distress costs.
- WACC with taxes decreases as leverage increases.
- Firms balance the tax benefits of debt against the expected costs of financial distress.
- Agency benefits of debt, such as disciplining managers, are also considered.
- Low asymmetric information costs of debt are weighed against the costs of financial distress.
- Agency costs of debt, like excessive risk-taking or debt overhang, must be considered.

# Portfolio Theory Concepts

Portfolio theory helps determine the expected return and volatility of a combination of assets. By considering weights, expected returns, and covariances, investors can construct an optimal portfolio based on their risk tolerance.

## Key points

- The expected return of a portfolio is the weighted-average of the expected returns of the individual stocks.
- To determine portfolio volatility, the variance of each stock, portfolio weights, and the covariance or correlation between stocks are needed.
- Short selling involves selling a stock you do not own, betting on its price dropping, and buying it back later.
- A risk-free investment, like Treasury bills, offers a known return with zero volatility and is uncorrelated with other investments.
- Leverage occurs when an investor borrows money to invest more than 100% of their wealth in the stock market.
- The optimal portfolio is found at the tangency point between the investment opportunity set and the efficient frontier.

# Bond Characteristics And Yields

This section outlines the characteristics of bonds, including different types, cash flows, and pricing conventions. It also discusses bond yields and the economic significance of the yield curve's shape.

## Key points

- A bond is a borrowing arrangement where the borrower issues an IOU to the investor.
- Bond cash flows consist of coupons (if paid) and the face value received at maturity.
- Coupon bonds make periodic interest payments, while zero-coupon bonds do not.
- Bond prices and yields move in opposite directions.
- The yield on a bond is the Internal Rate of Return (IRR) achieved when holding it to maturity.
- An upward sloping yield curve suggests future short-term interest rates will rise.
- A downward sloping yield curve suggests future short-term interest rates will fall and is a negative economic signal.

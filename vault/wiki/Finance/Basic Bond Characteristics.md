# Basic Bond Characteristics

A bond represents a borrowing agreement where the issuer provides an IOU to the investor. Key terms include face value, coupon rate, and the difference between coupon and zero-coupon bonds.

## Key points

- A bond is a borrowing arrangement where the borrower sells an IOU to the investor.
- Coupon bonds involve the issuer making interest payments, often semiannual.
- Face value is the principal amount paid back at maturity and is used to calculate interest payments.
- Zero coupon bonds do not involve any coupon payments.
- US Treasury Bills have maturities between 1 and 12 months and have no coupon payments.
- US Treasury Notes and Bonds have maturities of 2 years or more and are coupon bonds.

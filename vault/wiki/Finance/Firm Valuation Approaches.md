# Firm Valuation Approaches

Firm value is determined by the cash flows generated by all its assets, discounted by a rate that reflects the risk of those assets. Equity value, conversely, is the residual claim on cash flows remaining after all debt obligations are met.

## Key points

- Firm value is determined by cash flows from all assets and a risk-adjusted discount rate.
- Equity value is determined by cash flows remaining after debt holders are paid and a risk-adjusted discount rate.
- Equity holders have a residual claim on income after all obligations, including debt, are paid.
- The equity cost of capital ($r_E$) equals the risk-free rate plus a risk premium specific to the stock's risk.
- The Dividend Discount Model (DDM) prices a stock as the present value of its future dividends.
- The Discounted Free Cash Flow (DCF) model values equity based on the present value of FCFs available to equity holders.
- Multiples valuation compares the target firm to comparable companies using relevant financial ratios.

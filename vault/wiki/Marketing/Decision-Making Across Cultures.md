# Decision-Making Across Cultures

Different cultures approach decision-making using distinct styles regarding hierarchy, speed, and consensus. Misunderstandings between cultural styles can lead to misinterpretations of commitment and sincerity.

## Key points

- U.S. decision-making is top-down, fast, and revisable.
- Germany combines a hierarchical structure with a need for consensus, leading to slow, fixed decisions.
- Japan uses a hierarchical system combined with ultra-consensus achieved through processes like *ringi* and *nemawashi*.
- When dealing with consensual cultures, expect longer decision cycles and treat decisions as difficult to reverse.
- When dealing with top-down cultures, accept quick decisions from leadership and anticipate potential revisions.

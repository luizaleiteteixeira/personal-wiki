# Product Life Cycle Stages

The Product Life Cycle (PLC) is a model used for strategic planning that describes the stages a product goes through from introduction to decline. Each stage requires different marketing approaches depending on the product's market acceptance and competitive environment.

## Key points

- The stages of the PLC are Introduction, Growth, Maturity, and Decline.
- Introduction stage requires high promotion spending to build distribution channels and target early adopters.
- Growth stage involves competitors entering the market while promotional spending remains high and profits begin.
- Maturity stage focuses on competition, often encouraging existing customers to buy more frequently.
- Decline stage necessitates a decision to prune, reposition, or pursue a niche strategy, as reversal is difficult.
- Elasticity changes throughout the cycle, becoming more sensitive during periods of high competition.
- Introduction Stage involves heavy promotional spending to build awareness and trial.
- Growth Stage sees competitors entering the market as category growth continues.
- Maturity Stage is characterized by slowing category growth and declining profits.
- Decline Stage occurs when category decline is irreversible, though revival is possible.

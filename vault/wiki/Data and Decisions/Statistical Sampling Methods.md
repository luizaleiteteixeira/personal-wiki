# Statistical Sampling Methods

Statistical sampling involves using a subset of data to make inferences about a larger population when a full census is impractical. Several methods exist to ensure the sample accurately represents the target population.

## Key points

- Simple Random Sampling is considered the gold standard for selecting a sample.
- Stratified Sampling applies Simple Random Sampling within predefined subsets of the population.
- Cluster Sampling is used when groups are randomly selected, and SRS is applied within those groups.
- Bias can arise if the sampling frame is not representative or if self-selection occurs.
- The Law of Large Numbers suggests the sample mean approaches the true population mean as sample size increases.
- The Central Limit Theorem states that the distribution of the sample mean approaches a normal distribution with a large enough random sample size.

# Oligopoly Market Structure

Oligopoly is a market structure characterized by a small number of competitors facing high barriers to entry. In this setting, firms must strategically consider the actions of rivals, leading to complex pricing and output decisions.

## Key points

- Conditions include large fixed costs, resource limitations, and policy restrictions.
- Bertrand competition involves firms competing primarily on price with identical products.
- In Bertrand competition, price drops until $P=MC$ (Nash equilibrium), often leading to zero profit.
- To sustain profitability, firms can differentiate products, lock in customers, or reduce price transparency.
- When capacity is limited (e.g., hotel rooms), the Cournot model applies, where firms compete on quantity.
- Firms can attempt tacit collusion by matching prices, though explicit collusion is often illegal.
- The quantity produced is greater in Cournot than in Bertrand, but less than in a monopoly.

# Third-Degree Pricing Discrimination

Egon Zehnder introduced third-degree pricing discrimination to cater to different client types, allowing them to charge varying fees based on company size and segment. This strategy enabled them to serve smaller, budget-constrained companies while maximizing revenue from larger, less price-sensitive clients.

## Key points

- The service initially used a single price across all clients.
- Third-degree pricing discrimination was introduced based on company size and segment.
- Startups and digital firms paid lower fees, while large listed companies paid more.
- Conditions for segment pricing were met: heterogeneity in willingness to pay, ability to identify groups, and ability to separate segments.
- Threats included customer anger over differential pricing and competition maintaining uniform rates.
- The strategy successfully expanded the client base and increased overall revenue.
- The company used a "menu of services" to allow clients to self-select their pricing tier.
- The "high type" clients, like large corporations, had a higher willingness to pay for comprehensive service.
- "Low type" clients, such as startups, could afford the lower-priced version.
- This situation is an example of versioning in second-degree price discrimination.

# Monopolistic Competition Market Structure

Monopolistic competition involves many sellers offering differentiated products in a market with low barriers to entry. In the short run, firms act like a monopolist, but in the long run, competition drives profits to zero.

## Key points

- Products are differentiated but closely related or substitutable.
- Large numbers of buyers and sellers exist, and firms have some price-making power.
- Short-run equilibrium occurs where price equals the monopolist's condition (MR=MC).
- Long-run equilibrium is reached when MR=MC and Price equals Average Cost (AC), resulting in no economic profit.
- Sustained profitability often requires barriers to entry or a unique advantage.
- The market dynamic involves companies leaving or entering based on short-run profits.

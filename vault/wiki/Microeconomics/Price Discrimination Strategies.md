# Price Discrimination Strategies

Price discrimination involves charging different prices to different consumers without those differences being due to cost variations. The goal is to capture consumer surplus and eliminate deadweight loss.

## Key points

- First-degree (perfect) discrimination charges each consumer their Willingness To Pay (WTP).
- Third-degree discrimination involves setting different prices for distinct groups based on their WTP.
- Conditions for successful price discrimination include different buyer WTP, ability to identify WTP, and ability to segment buyers.
- Second-degree discrimination uses product versions (menu) to allow buyers to reveal their own value type.
- Bundling and quantity discounts are strategies used to decrease deadweight loss.

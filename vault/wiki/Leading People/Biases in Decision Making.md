# Biases in Decision Making

Wise decisions require overcoming cognitive limitations, as humans often rely on mental shortcuts that introduce systematic biases. These biases can lead to flawed judgments regarding probability, value, and causality.

## Key points

- Bounded Rationality suggests people often "satisfice" due to limits on time and attention.
- Availability bias causes vivid or easily recalled events to be judged as more probable.
- Anchoring bias shows that initial information disproportionately influences subsequent estimates.
- Prospect Theory demonstrates that choices are sensitive to whether they are framed as gains or losses.
- Fundamental Attribution Error involves over-attributing others' behavior to personality while understating situational factors.
- Confirmation Bias leads individuals to seek and favor information confirming existing beliefs.
- To challenge biases, one must use data, consider alternative perspectives, and actively seek disconfirming information.

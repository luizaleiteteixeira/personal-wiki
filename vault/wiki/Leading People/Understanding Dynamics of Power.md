# Understanding Dynamics of Power

Power is defined as the asymmetric control over resources and outcomes, which can stem from formal roles, relationships, or personal attributes. The exercise of power has distinct effects on individuals, including increased confidence but also potential ethical risks.

## Key points

- Power is the asymmetric control of resources and outcomes.
- Signals of power can include title, clothing, and obedience from others.
- The Milgram experiment demonstrated obedience to legitimate authority, especially under pressure.
- Effects of power include increased independent focus and decreased interdependent focus.
- The Power Amplification Effect magnifies subtle gestures from subordinates.
- Ethical risks include Noble Cause Corruption, where ends justify unethical means.
- Counteracting negative effects requires cultivating perspective-taking and a beginner's mindset.
